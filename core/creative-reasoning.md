# Stage 4a: Explore Three Directions

Creativity is contextual problem solving, not novelty. Before converging, write three genuinely different directions to `.design/directions.md`. A direction that exists only in reasoning did not happen.

## What Must Differ

Vary structural variables, never only color:

- operational vs editorial character
- dense vs spacious rhythm
- object-centric vs workflow-centric information architecture
- persistent vs contextual navigation
- table or list vs canvas or visual data presentation
- quiet vs expressive typography

## Direction Entry Format

One paragraph plus a short spec per direction:

```markdown
## Direction A: <two-word name>
<One paragraph: the idea, why it fits this product, what the first screen would feel like.>
- Hierarchy: <what the eye sees first and why>
- Navigation: <model>
- Density: <dense / balanced / spacious>
- Typography: <role of type in the identity>
- Data: <how information is presented>
- Signature candidate: <one product-specific move>
- Risk: <what could go wrong>
```

## Selection

Score each direction 1 to 5 on product fit, user fit, workflow clarity, distinctiveness, scalability, accessibility, and implementation realism. Pick the highest total, or synthesize two if the synthesis is stronger and still coherent. Record the pick and the reason at the end of the file.

## Controlled Risk

Most interactions stay predictable. Allow one to three meaningful creative risks on a major surface, in composition, typography, data presentation, navigation, rhythm, or storytelling. Never risk destructive actions, accessibility semantics, or familiar controls without a usability reason.

Prefer a clever information layout over a decorative effect. Prefer a useful interaction over a visual gimmick.
